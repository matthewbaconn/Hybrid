What the research says

In 2025, researchers followed 5,205 runners from 87 countries for 18 months using their Garmin data: about 588,000 runs. Over a third got a running injury, mostly overuse. Compared with runs within 10% of the runner's longest run of the previous 30 days, runs 10–30% longer had a 64% higher injury rate, runs 30–100% longer had a 52% higher rate, and runs more than double had more than twice the rate. Weekly measures, including the acute:chronic workload ratio and week-to-week change, showed no clear link to injury in the same data.

Isn't it the 10% rule?

The classic "add no more than 10% a week" rule was tested in a randomized trial with novice runners: a slower, 10%-a-week program did not reduce injuries compared with a faster one. A separate study of novice runners found more injuries in those who increased weekly distance by over 30% in two weeks. Weekly totals still matter, just less precisely than the old rule suggests. The simplest takeaway from all three: build your long run in small steps, and avoid one-off runs far beyond what you've done recently.

How to use this

If your planned run is a big jump, split the difference: run about 10% beyond your recent longest, and add the rest over the next few weeks. Lifting, bad sleep and a stressful week all add load the distance doesn't show. This is a training guide, not medical advice. If something hurts, get it checked by a physio or doctor.
