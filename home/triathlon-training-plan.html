Most of it should feel easy

A study of nine triathletes through an 18-week Ironman build sorted every session into three heart-rate zones. Racing happened mostly in the moderate middle zone, yet the athletes who spent more of their training in the easy zone (zone 1, below the first threshold) finished faster. A lot of moderate riding was linked to slower overall times. It's a small study, so treat it as a direction, not a law: keep the easy days easy so the hard days can be hard.

Heads up: that study's "zone 1" is easy, conversational effort. Many watches call that same effort "zone 2". In the plan, "conversational" is the word that matters.

What each session does

  • Long ride. The endurance the whole race sits on. It grows week by week, with fueling practice once it passes about 75 minutes.
  • Brick run. A short easy run straight off the long ride, every other week in build and peak, so your legs learn the switch.
  • Bike intervals. Harder efforts that raise the power you can hold. Spin-ups in base, longer race-effort blocks at peak.
  • Swims. Two or three shorter swims a week build feel for the water. In build and peak, one becomes a continuous swim near race distance with sighting.
  • Long run and tempo run. Run durability for the last leg, plus one threshold session from build onward.
  • Strength. Optional, never on the long ride day. Heavy and explosive strength work can improve running and cycling economy when it's added to endurance training.

How the week is put together

  • The long ride gets its own day, with the brick run on it and no lifting.
  • The long run sits next to it, so your big endurance load lands on back-to-back days and the rest of the week stays free for quality.
  • Hard sessions are spread out. Bike intervals and the tempo run go as far from each other and from the long day as your days allow. Never more than two sessions in a day.
  • Every 4th week is lighter, then the plan tapers (longer for 70.3 and 140.6) so you arrive fresh.
