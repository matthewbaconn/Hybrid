Why the running matters this much

A HYROX race is 8 × 1 km runs, each followed by a station. In a 2025 study of 11 recreational athletes doing a full simulated race, about 59% of the finish time was spent running (51 of 87 minutes on average). Faster finishers had a higher VO2max and did more endurance training. Grip strength, muscle mass and resistance training volume did not line up with finish time. It's one small study, but the message is clear: your engine decides a lot of your result.

That doesn't mean skip the strength. A 2025 review of HYROX demands calls the aerobic system the foundation that lets you recover between stations, with strength, muscular endurance and power carrying the sleds, lunges and wall balls. You need both, placed so they don't wreck each other.

What each session does

  • HYROX intervals. 1 km at race effort, straight into a station, repeated. Running on tired legs is the skill HYROX tests, so this is the most race-specific session. It grows from 4 rounds in base to 8 at peak.
  • 1 km repeats at threshold. Eight 1 km runs make up half the race. This rehearses the rhythm on fresh legs.
  • Long run, easy. Builds the aerobic base that helps you recover between stations. It caps at 9 miles: HYROX doesn't need marathon long runs.
  • HYROX strength. Front squats, deadlifts, sandbag lunges, push press and wall balls. Strong legs and grip make the sleds feel smaller.
  • Station skills (6 days a week). Moderate rounds focused on smooth technique, so each station costs less on race day.

How the week is put together

  • The long run gets its own day. No lifting on it.
  • Hard days are spread out. The HYROX intervals and the 1 km repeats go as far from each other and from the long run as your days allow.
  • Every 4th week is lighter, then the last week tapers to about 60% so you arrive fresh.
  • No sled? Push a weighted prowler, or swap in 20 hard kettlebell swings.
