Recovery

Tired? Your plan already knows.

A 20-second morning check-in plus your watch's sleep, resting heart rate and HRV give you a readiness score. On low days, the workout gets shorter or easier.

  • Readiness, sleep and soreness at a glance

The stuff other apps skip.

  1. 01

    No watch? It still works.

    Tap "Start with my phone" and a voice coach calls every rep, keeps time, and tracks distance by GPS if you want. Pace by feel with the talk test, rate how hard it was from 1 to 10, and the plan adapts to that. A watch makes it sharper. It isn't required.

  2. 02

    Pickleball counts.

    Soccer, yoga, a hike, a spin class. Whatever you did, Mileset figures out what it was, how hard it was and how it fits your week. If it replaced an easy run, it gets checked off. You get a plain-English summary after every activity.

  3. 03

    Training that knows your cycle.

    Optional for women: your phase, your symptoms and your own pattern steer the hard days. It stays on your phone. It never touches our servers.

  4. 04

    Race in 10 days? Still yes.

    Signed up late? Mileset builds a taper from where you are now instead of telling you it's too soon.

  5. 05

    Treadmill or outside, your call.

    Pick per run. Treadmill gets speeds in mph and no heat adjustment. Your watch still gets one workout, not two.

Why I built this

I'm 21, in nursing school, and I work a shift that starts at 4:30 in the morning. I played football, I lift, and I ran the Marine Corps Marathon. Every plan I found assumed running was my whole life. None of them knew what to do with leg day, a bad night of sleep, or a week where school ate everything.

So I built the plan I wanted: one that bends around a real schedule and still gets you to the start line ready. If something's off or missing, email me at milesetapp@gmail.com. I read every one.

Matthew
