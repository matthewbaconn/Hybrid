Will running kill my gains?

Less than gym lore says. A 2012 meta-analysis did find that adding endurance work blunted strength and muscle gains, and running more so than cycling, with bigger effects at higher frequency and longer sessions. A 2022 update with more studies found no meaningful loss of muscle size or max strength from combining the two, but explosive strength still took a hit, more so when both were done in the same session. In practice: keep your easy runs easy, don't jam a hard run and heavy legs into one session, and give a half marathon block 10 to 16 weeks so mileage climbs gradually.

Lifting makes you a better runner, too

Reviews of strength training in runners and cyclists find that heavy and explosive strength work improves running economy and performance without the bulk people worry about. That's why the planner keeps you lifting all the way to race week and only trims sets in the taper.

Where the lifts go, and why

  • On hard-run days first. Lift on the same day as your intervals or tempo, after the run, ideally a few hours later. Your easy days stay truly easy, which is where most of your aerobic base comes from.
  • Never on the long-run day. The long run is the key session of a half marathon block. Show up to it fresh.
  • No leg day the day before the long run. Heavy lower-body lifting can leave your legs heavier and your running less economical for a day or two. Upper body goes there instead.
  • Lighter in the last two weeks. Drop a set from each lift and keep the weight. You keep the strength and arrive with fresh legs.
